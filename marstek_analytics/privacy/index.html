Battery ETASupport

Privacy policy

Effective 24 November 2026

Battery ETA does not sell or share personal data. It has no sign-up, no advertising and no tracking. It sends anonymous usage statistics, which you can turn off in Settings. Shared history, which you turn on yourself, keeps your devices' battery history on the developer's server in a form only your devices can read.

What the app stores on your device

  • The battery IP address, port and reserve you enter.
  • Recent readings from your battery, used to draw the app and its widgets.
  • The address and outage group you pick on the Address screen, and the outage schedule for that group.

This data stays in the app's storage on your phone or tablet. Deleting the app deletes all of it. Battery readings leave the device only through shared history, described below, and only encrypted.

Network use

The app reads the battery only at its address on your local Wi‑Fi. Find battery sends one device query to each address on your Wi‑Fi network and nowhere else.

The Address screen is optional. When you use it, the app sends the address you type or pick to your region's public outage service to find your outage group. In Kyiv city and in Dnipropetrovsk oblast, that is YASNO (app.yasno.ua). In Lviv, Mykolaiv, Zhytomyr and Poltava oblasts, it is the oblast's power company (power-api.loe.lviv.ua, off.energy.mk.ua, www.ztoe.com.ua, www.poe.pl.ua). In 12 other oblasts, Kyiv oblast among them, it is the alerts.energy address directory. In the remaining oblasts the app sends no address.

Requests to alerts.energy go through the developer's server on Cloudflare Workers, battery-eta-proxy.mlapin.workers.dev. The server keeps each reply for about 24 hours, keyed by what you searched for, so repeat lookups don't reach the directory. To limit request rates, it also keeps a salted hash of your IP address for about an hour. The server's code writes no IP address or search text to its logs.

The app downloads outage schedules from YASNO (app.yasno.ua) and from the developer's feed on GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com). These requests name your power company and, if you picked one in Settings, the schedule source. They never name your address.

Each of these services and Cloudflare sees your IP address. alerts.energy sees only the developer's server. Each service's own privacy policy covers its requests.

When you tap Use my location, the system asks for your permission and gives the app your location once. The phone's geocoding service turns it into a street name: Apple's on iPhone and iPad, and Google's on most Android phones. The app keeps only the city, street and house text. Your coordinates are not stored or sent anywhere else.

The app also sends anonymous usage statistics and, if you turn it on, shared history, both described below. It contacts no other server.

Anonymous usage statistics

The app sends a few events to PostHog, on its EU servers (eu.i.posthog.com and eu-assets.i.posthog.com), so the developer can see where setup fails and which features people use. Examples: the app was opened, Find battery found a battery or found none, a connection failed and in which category, how many widgets are on the screen, whether the outage schedule comes from YASNO or the developer's feed.

Each event carries:

  • a random install ID and a random session ID;
  • the app's name, ID, version and build number, and the PostHog library version;
  • the system name and version, the device type, maker and model, and the screen size;
  • whether the app runs on an emulator and whether it came from an app store;
  • the time zone, the system language and the app language;
  • whether you are on Wi‑Fi or cellular;
  • a few PostHog settings and status values.

On Android, each event also carries the system region, the device code name, the screen density, whether the connection runs over Bluetooth, and the system's user-agent text, which names the device and system build. On iPhone and iPad, it also says whether the app came from TestFlight, whether it runs on a Mac and, on a foldable iPhone, whether it is folded.

The install ID is made by the app, is deleted when you delete the app and is not linked to you, your Apple or Google account or your battery.

The app never sends your battery's IP address, serial number or readings, your street or house, your GPS location, your outage group or any text you type. PostHog drops your IP address and does not look up your location from it.

To stop sending statistics, turn off Share anonymous usage stats in Settings.

Shared history

Shared history is off until you add a second device in Settings › Shared history, by scanning a QR code and checking a matching code on both screens. Up to 8 devices then share one battery's history, events and temperature, and a device away from home shows the last charge read at home.

The devices exchange this data through Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore and Cloud Functions in Belgium, europe-west1). Each device encrypts the data before it leaves, with keys that exist only on your paired devices. The developer and Google cannot read it. The encrypted data includes the readings, the events, the battery's IP address and port, and the device names.

While the app is open on several devices at home, only one of them reads the battery. It sends each new reading, encrypted, through Firebase Cloud Messaging to the other devices, which show it on screen and in their widgets. For this, each device in a group gets a push token from Firebase and subscribes to a topic whose name comes from your group's key. On iPhone and iPad, Apple's push service delivers these messages. When a device leaves the group, the app unsubscribes it and deletes its push token.

To deliver the data, the server also holds, unencrypted:

  • a random group ID and, for each device, a random account ID made by Firebase Authentication and the device's public key;
  • for each device and day, the date, the time of its last upload, an upload counter and the number of the key it was encrypted with;
  • which device reads the battery at the moment, as its account ID, and until when, next to the last reading, encrypted;
  • while a device is being added, a pairing record that lasts about 10 minutes.

Upload times show when a device was on and online, for example that the device at home had power on a given day. Nothing on the server names you, your address, your Apple or Google account or your battery.

The account ID is anonymous: there is no sign-up, email or password. To check that requests come from the real app, Firebase App Check asks Apple (App Attest or DeviceCheck) or Google Play Integrity about the app; this sends no data of yours to the developer. Firebase sees your IP address for each request, to prevent abuse.

To leave, swipe this device's row in Settings › Shared history and tap Remove. The app then deletes this device's history and its anonymous account from the server. A device removed from another device, or deleted with the app still in the group, stops uploading, and its data stays until the server deletes it on its own: each day 29 days after its date, and a whole group, with the record of which device reads the battery, 60 days after its last upload.

Third parties

The app includes the PostHog SDK for the statistics above, the Firebase App Check and Firebase Cloud Messaging SDKs for shared history, and no advertising SDK. Google's Firebase privacy terms cover its part of shared history. If you turned on sharing with app developers in iOS settings, or usage and diagnostics on Android, Apple or Google may send crash reports to the developer. Their privacy policies cover that.

Children

The app is not meant for children and collects nothing beyond what this page describes.
