Battery ETASupport

Privacy policy

Effective 6 November 2026

Battery ETA does not collect, share or sell any personal data. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.

What the app stores on your device

  • The battery IP address, port and reserve you enter.
  • Recent readings from your battery, used to draw the app and its widgets.
  • The address and outage group you pick on the Address screen, and the outage schedule for that group.

This data stays in the app's storage on your iPhone or iPad. Battery readings never leave the device. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

Network use

The app reads the battery only at its address on your local Wi‑Fi. Find battery sends one device query to each address on your Wi‑Fi network and nowhere else.

The Address screen is optional. When you use it, the app sends the city, street and house text you type to the public address directory of the grid operator you picked, to find your outage group: YASNO (app.yasno.ua) for Kyiv and Dnipro, Lvivoblenergo (power-api.loe.lviv.ua), Mykolaivoblenergo (off.energy.mk.ua), Zhytomyroblenergo (ztoe.com.ua) or Poltavaoblenergo (poe.pl.ua). Each operator's own privacy policy covers these requests.

For the other oblasts with schedules you pick the group from a list, and nothing is sent. If you tap Find by address there, the app sends the city, street and house text you type to battery-eta-proxy.mlapin.workers.dev, a Battery ETA service on Cloudflare. It passes the text to the alerts.energy address directory and returns the outage groups for that house. The service writes no log of what you typed. It caches each directory answer for one day, so the same search does not go to alerts.energy twice. To limit requests, it counts them for one hour under a one-way hash of your IP address and then deletes the count. It does not store the IP address itself. Cloudflare's and alerts.energy's privacy policies cover their part of these requests.

The app then downloads the public outage schedule for your operator, from YASNO or from raw.githubusercontent.com (GitHub), where Battery ETA publishes the schedules it collects from the operators' sites. These requests carry no personal data. GitHub's privacy statement covers the download.

When you tap Use my location, iOS asks for your permission and gives the app your location once. Apple's geocoding service turns it into a street name, and the app keeps only that street and house text. Your coordinates are not stored or sent anywhere else.

The app contacts no other server.

Third parties

The app has no third-party code, analytics or advertising SDKs. If you turned on sharing with app developers in iOS settings, Apple may send crash reports to the developer. Apple's privacy policy covers that.

Children

The app collects no data from anyone, including children.
